# Case Study: Perioperative Workflow Intelligence

**Domain:** Anesthesiology / Operating Room Operations
**SME Profile:** Board-certified anesthesiologist, 12+ years clinical practice across academic and community hospital settings
**Engagement:** 4 structured sessions (60 min each) + async follow-up
**Status:** Complete — insight brief and GTM canvas produced

---

## Why This Domain

Anesthesiology sits at the intersection of three acute pressures in modern healthcare:
1. **Patient safety** — errors in perioperative care have outsized consequences
2. **Workflow complexity** — the OR involves more handoffs, more roles, and more time-critical decisions than almost any other clinical setting
3. **Documentation burden** — the gap between what clinicians know and what gets recorded is enormous

This made it a high-signal domain for the framework: an expert with deep tacit knowledge, a complex workflow with multiple friction points, and a market that has tried (and largely failed) to solve it with technology.

## Knowledge Pillars Identified

1. **Pharmacokinetics in Practice** — how drugs behave differently in real patients vs. textbook cases
2. **Hemodynamic Interpretation** — reading physiological signals and knowing when to act vs. observe
3. **OR Communication Norms** — unwritten rules of how the team communicates under pressure
4. **Handoff Architecture** — the formal and informal structure of patient transitions between care phases
5. **Risk Stratification** — how anesthesiologists assess and communicate patient risk before surgery

---

## Priority Problems Identified

### P1: Medication Reconciliation at Handoff (Urgent / High Market Signal)
No standardized protocol for communicating medication state during patient handoffs between OR and recovery. Current workaround: verbal summary, often incomplete. Risk: adverse drug events.

**Market validation:** Confirmed — medication reconciliation errors are documented as one of the leading causes of preventable harm in hospital settings.

### P2: Pre-Op Decision Documentation Gap (Important / Latent)
The decisions made in the 15 minutes before surgery are often the most consequential, but almost none are captured in the EMR. Clinicians rely on memory and personal notes.

**Market validation:** Adjacent — some EMR vendors are building pre-op modules, but none capture the actual decision rationale.

### P3: Stability Signal Communication (Latent / High Pillar Intersection)
Experienced anesthesiologists read subtle patient stability signals that newer clinicians miss. There's no systematic way to transfer this intuition during supervision or handoff.

**Market validation:** Emerging — explainability in clinical AI is an active research area, but no commercial product addresses this directly.
