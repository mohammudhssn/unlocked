# The SME Partnership Model

> Not consulting. Co-building.

The partnership model is the highest-leverage engagement structure in Unlocked. Instead of trading time for money on a consulting retainer, you build shared equity in the output. One successful micro-tool at $3k/month across ten partnerships is $30k/month recurring — without adding a single new client conversation.

---

## The Core Thesis

Most knowledge workers have been told their expertise only has value in exchange for their time. Hourly. Retainer. Deliverable. Always transactional, always capped by hours in the day.

DIF gives SMEs a different frame: productize the knowledge once and earn forever. The gap between "I know this" and "this is a product" is not skill — it's structure. We provide the structure.

**The Unlocked pitch to an SME:**

> "You've spent years building expertise that nobody can buy in a scalable form. I've built a system that extracts that knowledge, validates whether there's a market for it, and turns it into a product that earns while you sleep. I run the whole process. You bring your knowledge and your network. We split what it produces."

## What Each Party Brings

### The SME Brings

**1. Context (irreplaceable)**
Their domain knowledge is the unfair advantage. No amount of desk research replicates 15 years of clinical practice, or 20 years of construction project management. The knowledge lives in them — and DIF is the extraction system.

**2. Credibility (validation)**
Their reputation validates the output. A nurse scheduling tool built with a nurse carries more trust than the same tool built by an outsider. Their title and experience is a GTM asset.

**3. Community (distribution)**
Every SME has a domain peer network — LinkedIn connections, Slack communities, conferences, professional associations. Their 2,000 domain peers are all potential users or buyers. This is targeted distribution no ad spend can match.

### You Bring

**1. Structure (the methodology)**
DIF. The question bank, the session architecture, the synthesis layer, the output decision tree. The expert has never been asked to think about their knowledge in this structured way — the process itself generates insight they didn't know they had.

**2. Speed (AI leverage)**
The AI stack compresses what would normally take months of research into 2 weeks. Claude handles synthesis, enrichment, and first drafts. You review and direct. The SME talks. The output appears.

**3. Build (execution)**
You ship the product. Micro-tool: Claude API + form UI, 2–4 weeks. Course: curriculum + platform, 2–3 weeks. Productized service: SOP + delivery framework, days. The SME doesn't have to touch the build.

## Deal Structures

### Structure 1: Revenue Share (Tools, Services, Courses)

**Best for:** Micro-tools, productized services, SOP packs, courses

- You run the full engagement
- Co-build the output
- **50/50 split** on all revenue from the output
- You handle product maintenance and distribution strategy
- SME handles domain credibility and their network distribution

**Documentation:**
- Simple rev share agreement (see template below)
- Stripe or equivalent payment processor
- Clear definition of what the "product" is and what counts as revenue

**Why 50/50 works:** The SME's knowledge + network is irreplaceable. Your structure + speed + build is irreplaceable. Neither works without the other. Equal split removes negotiation friction and signals a genuine partnership.

---

### Structure 2: Equity (Startups)

**Best for:** Track 5 ventures — funded startups with $100M+ market potential

- Co-founder structure from day one
- SME takes domain/technical title (Chief Medical Officer, Chief Domain Officer, etc.)
- You take execution (CEO or CTO depending on the build)
- Initial equity: negotiate based on who brings what to the founding moment
- Typical range for SME in a DIF-originated startup: 20–40% depending on their ongoing involvement

**Documentation:**
- Founder agreement before any significant build
- Vesting schedule (standard 4-year, 1-year cliff)
- IP assignment — all DIF outputs from the engagement become company IP

**Important:** Document the partnership before the first session, not after the first pitch. Verbal agreements don't survive investor due diligence.

---

### Structure 3: Hybrid (Service → Equity Ladder)

**Best for:** Engagements that start as a service but have startup potential

- Begin with a Domain Intelligence Session at a reduced rate ($500–1,000)
- If a startup opportunity is identified, convert to co-founder structure
- The reduced session fee is the "price of discovery" — not a consulting fee
- The equity is the upside if the opportunity is real

This structure lowers the barrier for the SME to say yes. They're not committing to an equity split upfront — they're committing to a conversation.

## The Pitch Framework

**Step 1: The hook (one line)**
> "I've built a system that turns expert knowledge into products. I'm looking for one person in [their domain] to test it with."

*Why this works:* It's curious, not salesy. It implies scarcity. It invites without pitching.

**Step 2: The proof (one story)**
Share the TAC case study — 4 sessions with an anesthesiologist, a $2M problem identified in plain sight, a SaaS product now being scoped. Make it concrete and specific.

*Why this works:* The Epiphany Bridge. You're not describing a methodology — you're sharing a story that makes the methodology obvious.

**Step 3: The frame shift**
> "The way most experts think about their knowledge: it's only valuable while they're in the room. The way we think about it: it's valuable forever, once it's structured."

*Why this works:* It reframes the expert's relationship to their own knowledge. Most have never been told their expertise could be productized. This opens a new possibility.

**Step 4: The offer**
> "I want to run the full process with you. I do the research, structure the sessions, and build whatever comes out of it. You bring your knowledge and your network. We split the revenue 50/50. If after 4 sessions we haven't found a validated opportunity, you keep everything and I work for free."

*Why this works:* The guarantee removes the risk. The upside is unlimited. The downside is nothing.

**Step 5: The next step**
> "The first step is a 30-minute call where I learn about your domain and you get a sense of whether this makes sense. No commitment from either side."

*Why this works:* The smallest possible ask. No pitch deck. No contract. Just a conversation.

## Qualifying the Right SMEs

Not every expert is the right partner. Use these criteria before extending a partnership offer:

**Green lights:**
- 10+ years in a specific domain (depth matters more than breadth)
- Active peer network (LinkedIn presence, conference presence, community involvement)
- Has a problem they've complained about for years without a solution
- Intellectually curious — they find the DIF process interesting, not threatening
- Has time for 4 × 60-minute sessions over 2–3 weeks

**Yellow flags (explore further):**
- Currently employed full-time without entrepreneurial interest (less likely to co-build)
- Domain is highly regulated without regulatory pathway identified
- Network is small or not digitally reachable

**Red flags:**
- Expects a guaranteed outcome before sessions are done
- Wants to retain full IP control before partnership is agreed
- Has tried to productize before and isn't open about why it didn't work

## After the Partnership: The Distribution Play

The SME's network is the launch channel. Structure this explicitly:

**Week 1 post-launch:**
- SME sends a personal note to their top 20 domain connections about the product
- You write the email/post — they send it from their account
- Expect 10–20% response rate from a warm personal send

**Week 2–4:**
- SME shares a LinkedIn post about the process ("What 4 conversations revealed about my domain")
- You write it — they post it
- Their domain peers see it → curiosity → next SME referral

**Ongoing:**
- Every user of the product is a potential case study
- Every case study is content for the next SME pitch
- Every SME referral is the next engagement

The distribution machine builds itself.
